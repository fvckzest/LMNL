# LMNL Managed Ticketing Pilot — Washington legal and tax research

**Research date:** August 13, 2026
**Status:** Primary-source research, not legal or tax advice. This note does not determine the tax treatment of a particular event or the legal status of any person. Obtain Washington attorney and CPA advice before sales open.

## Scope and assumed facts

This note addresses one paid, in-person Washington **Pilot Event**: one all-in USD general-admission price, one date and venue fixed before sales open, payment collected only into LMNL's own Square account, no buyer-requested refunds, full refund on **Pilot Event Cancellation**, and a manual **Recipient Settlement** to one named **Settlement Recipient** within five business days. The Settlement Recipient is also an LMNL Admin; that administrative authority does not itself create a ticket-revenue right. The governing product terms are in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

This is deliberately not a marketplace or self-service-host analysis. It also does not decide venue-specific permits, alcohol, food, music/public-performance, security, occupancy, accessibility, insurance, or local tax/licensing rules; those depend on the actual venue, city, event content, and parties.

## Bottom line before the pilot

1. **No-sales gate: Square's own terms prohibit LMNL from using its Square account to handle, process, or transmit funds for a third party.** Square also requires every processed card transaction to be a bona fide sale that accurately describes the goods/services LMNL provides. The pilot can use this account only if counsel confirms that LMNL truly has the contractual right and responsibility to sell the admission itself—not merely to process another person's ticket revenue and remit it. [Square Payment Terms §§23 and 36](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/payment). This is a contractual gate in addition to the Washington licensing analysis below.
2. **Do not assume every event admission is Washington retail-sales-taxable.** Washington sales tax applies to sales that meet the statutory definition of a retail sale. Listed recreational activities are taxable, but the statutory list and its exclusions show that the event's actual content and the party furnishing it matter. A normal “paid in-person event” description is not enough to classify the ticket. [RCW 82.08.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=82.08.020) imposes sales tax only on retail sales; [RCW 82.04.050(15)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=82.04.050) and [WAC 458-20-183](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-183) identify the particular recreational activities that are retail sales and state that other tax results require all facts and circumstances.
3. **If the ticket is taxable, remove sales tax from the settlement base before any 10%/90% split and remit it.** Washington treats sales tax collected as trust funds, requires the seller to remit the correct amount, and requires it to be separately stated on the buyer's receipt even when the advertised price includes tax. [Washington DOR, Retail sales tax](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/retail-sales-tax); [WAC 458-20-107](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-107). The current formula—completed payments less refunds, chargebacks, and Square fees—does not expressly exclude separately stated sales tax. It must be changed operationally (and preferably in the Pilot Event Agreement) if tax applies.
4. **The all-in price is a federal live-event-ticket rule, not just a good practice.** FTC Rule 16 CFR Part 464 prohibits offering, displaying, or advertising a live-event ticket price without clearly and conspicuously disclosing the total price; the total must be more prominent than other pricing information. Before consent to pay, disclose excluded government charges and the final payment amount. Do not misrepresent a fee's purpose, amount, or refundability. [16 CFR Part 464](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-464).
5. **LMNL is the Square Seller and bears the immediate payment-network risk.** Square describes the account holder as the Seller, remits funds to that Seller, and can hold, debit, or charge back funds. A later settlement agreement does not move that exposure to Square or make the Settlement Recipient the Square merchant. [Square Payment Terms](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/payment); [Square dispute process](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/3882-payment-disputes-walkthrough).
6. **Obtain a Washington attorney's written determination before treating Recipient Settlement as unregulated pass-through money.** Washington broadly defines money transmission as receiving money to transmit or deliver it to another person, and puts the burden of an exclusion on the person claiming it. Federal FinCEN has an “integral to a sale of goods/services” principle, but that is federal MSB guidance—not a Washington licensure ruling for this model. [RCW 19.230.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.230.010); [RCW 19.230.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.230.020); [FinCEN ruling FIN-2014-R006](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/administrative-rulings/application-money-services-business-2).
7. **Treat tax/reporting classification as an open professional question, not something settled by the label “Recipient Share.”** Washington B&O tax is generally measured from gross income or gross proceeds, and federal Form 1099-K reports gross card payments without reductions for fees or refunds. The amount LMNL pays to the Settlement Recipient may instead be a deductible contractual expense, payment for services, or wages depending on facts; it is not automatically excluded merely because the product glossary calls it “not LMNL revenue.” [WAC 458-20-182](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-182); [IRS Form 1099-K instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099k).

## Confirmed requirements and direct consequences

### 1. Washington registration, B&O tax, and sales tax

- A Washington business needs a business license when, among other triggers, it collects retail sales tax, owes DOR taxes or fees, uses a trade name, hires employees, or has annual gross income of at least $12,000. A paid pilot can trigger registration even below $12,000 if it must collect sales tax or pay DOR tax. [Washington DOR, Business Licensing FAQs](https://dor.wa.gov/open-business/business-licensing-and-renewals-faqs).
- Washington B&O tax is a tax on business activity and generally uses gross receipts/gross income; it is not a buyer tax. The applicable classification may be retailing if the ticket is a retail sale, or another classification if it is not. [Washington DOR, B&O classification definitions](https://dor.wa.gov/open-business/apply-business-license/plan-taxes/business-and-occupation-bo-tax-classification-definitions); [RCW 82.04.290](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=82.04.290).
- The fact that LMNL deducts Square fees in the internal Net Ticket Revenue calculation does **not** generally reduce a taxable selling price or B&O-tax measure: Washington treats card-processing charges as the seller's cost of doing business. [WAC 458-20-108](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-108).
- If the ticket is a retail sale, the state rate is 6.5% plus the local rate. The correct local rate depends on where the buyer receives the service; for an in-person event, confirm the venue/location code using DOR's current lookup before configuring Square. [Washington DOR, Retail sales tax](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/retail-sales-tax); [Washington DOR, local rates](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/sales-use-tax-rates/local-sales-use-tax).
- If sales tax applies, it must be separately stated on the receipt or other instrument of sale. LMNL may advertise an all-in price only if it makes the required “tax included” disclosure; the receipt still needs the separate tax statement. [WAC 458-20-107](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-107).

**CPA confirmation required:** (a) whether the actual event is a taxable recreational/retail activity, a non-retail admission/service, or a bundled transaction; (b) which person is the seller/principal for B&O and sales-tax purposes; (c) whether the Recipient Share is included in LMNL's gross income before an expense/deduction, or has a defensible agency treatment; and (d) city B&O and local licensing. The listed recreational-services rule expressly cautions that results outside its examples depend on all facts and circumstances. [WAC 458-20-183](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-183).

**Marketplace-facilitator confirmation required:** If the Settlement Recipient is actually the seller of the admission, LMNL's operation may satisfy Washington's marketplace-facilitator definition: it contracts to facilitate a seller's product for consideration, communicates offer/acceptance, and performs one of the specified activities such as payment processing, listing, pricing, branding, taking orders, or customer service. The rule applies to retail sales, and the exact ticket classification still matters. If it applies, LMNL must collect/remit applicable tax on taxable retail sales, provide the seller monthly Washington-sales access within 15 days, and may owe service-and-other-activities B&O on its fee rather than retailing B&O on the facilitated sale. [WAC 458-20-282](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-282). This possible state tax role conflicts materially with Square's prohibition on processing funds for a third party, so it is another reason to use one genuine merchant/organizer of record for the pilot or obtain a different processor arrangement.

### 2. Buyer-facing price, refund, cancellation, and event disclosures

- Washington's Consumer Protection Act declares unfair or deceptive acts or practices in trade or commerce unlawful. Promising a fixed date/venue or cancellation refund and then not honoring those promises can create consumer-protection exposure even though this research found no general statutory “buyer remorse” refund right specifically for ordinary event tickets. [RCW 19.86.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.86.020).
- Federal Rule 16 CFR Part 464 applies specifically to live-event tickets: in every price representation, clearly and conspicuously disclose the total mandatory amount and make it at least as prominent as other pricing; before consent, disclose the final payment and the nature, purpose, and amount of any excluded government charge. Do not misrepresent a fee's refundability. [16 CFR Part 464](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-464).
- The all-in ticket price is compatible with the tax rule only if the buyer can tell whether tax is included and the final receipt separately states taxable sales tax when tax applies. [WAC 458-20-107](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=458-20-107).
- Square's terms independently require disclosure of the return/cancellation policy at purchase, require refunds/adjustments to be processed through the Square account under the Network Rules, and state that Square does not refund its processing fees. [Square Payment Terms §34](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/payment).
- A “final sale except event cancellation” policy does not prevent a cardholder from disputing a charge. Square says a refund policy has no bearing on a “not as described” dispute; Square can immediately withhold the disputed amount, and a lost dispute can leave LMNL with both the refund and nonrefunded processing fee. [Square, not-as-described disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8563-respond-to-disputes-for-not-as-described-or-defective-goods-or-services); [Square, managing disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/3882-payment-disputes-walkthrough).

**Reasonable risk-control recommendations (not a finding that a special ticket statute mandates each one):** Before payment, conspicuously show LMNL as the seller/contact; exact event name, date, start time, address/venue, general-admission capacity; all-in price and whether tax is included; the final-sale/no-buyer-refund rule; the precise cancellation trigger and full-refund promise; and any age, entry, accessibility, or conduct constraints. Capture affirmative checkout acceptance and retain the rendered terms/version and confirmation email. Square specifically identifies clear descriptions, policies, and proof of pre-purchase acknowledgment as useful dispute evidence. [Square, preventing disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6138-preventing-disputes); [Square, credit-not-processed disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8557-respond-to-credit-not-processed-disputes).

### 3. Pilot Event Agreement and Recipient Settlement

- Washington recognizes electronic records, signatures, and contracts; a record or contract is not denied legal effect solely because it is electronic. A signed PDF or attributable electronic signature can therefore be used for the Pilot Event Agreement. [RCW 1.80.060](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=1.80.060).
- Square's payment terms govern LMNL's relationship with Square: Square's Payment Services are for the Seller to accept customer payments for goods and services, fees are deducted from funds, and a payment can be charged back to LMNL's Square account. Square prohibits acting as a payment facilitator or handling, processing, or transmitting funds for a third party; it also prohibits assignment of card receivables/proceeds to a third party except as specified. [Square Payment Terms §§23 and 25](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/payment).

**Reasonable agreement controls:** state that LMNL, not the Settlement Recipient, is the disclosed ticket seller and Square Seller; identify the Recipient Share as LMNL's contractual payment obligation; define every input and time boundary for the settlement statement; exclude collected/remittable taxes from the split; state who bears Square fees, refunds, and later chargebacks; authorize a documented repayment or future-settlement offset for a post-settlement chargeback; require payment instructions and a receipt/confirmation reference; and state what happens if the event is cancelled, capacity/date/venue cannot be honored, or funds are held by Square. The agreement should not say that Square “holds the recipient's revenue” or that LMNL is merely forwarding buyer funds unless a Washington attorney supports that characterization.

### 4. Money-transmission and money-custody boundary

- Washington defines money transmission as receiving money or equivalent value to transmit, deliver, or instruct delivery to another person or location. A person engaged in it is a money transmitter. [RCW 19.230.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.230.010).
- Washington lists exclusions, but the statute says the claimant bears the burden of proving an exclusion or exception; it does not supply a broad ticket-platform/agent-of-payee exclusion on these facts. [RCW 19.230.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.230.020). DFI tells a business seeking an exclusion to include its exclusion argument and directs uncertain applicants to review the definition and contact DFI. [DFI Money Transmitter Licensing FAQ](https://dfi.wa.gov/money-services/licensing-frequently-asked-questions).
- Under federal FinCEN guidance, accepting and transmitting funds only as an integral part of selling goods or providing non-money-transmission services generally does not itself make a person a money transmitter; the determination is facts-and-circumstances specific. [FinCEN ruling FIN-2014-R006](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/administrative-rulings/application-money-services-business-2). This does **not** answer Washington licensing or custody law.

**Attorney confirmation required before launch:** whether LMNL's single-event, disclosed-merchant model and settlement agreement make the Recipient Settlement payment of LMNL's own contractual obligation, rather than receipt of buyer funds for transmission; whether any Washington money-transmitter, escrow, trust, agency, or fiduciary rule applies; and whether a written DFI interpretation/no-action request is appropriate. Do not launch on a conclusion that the federal integral-to-services principle automatically supplies a Washington exclusion.

**Practical custody control:** until that advice arrives, do not commingle the Recipient Share calculation with LMNL operating cash in the ledger. Maintain an event-level liability/clearing ledger, reserve enough cash for the cancellation-refund and likely-chargeback exposure, and make settlement only after a documented review of completed payments, refunds, disputes, Square fees, taxes, and the due date. These are prudent controls, not a conclusion that a particular trust account is legally required.

### 5. Chargebacks and refunds after settlement

- A cardholder can file a chargeback; Square places an immediate hold by withholding from the Square balance or debiting the linked bank account, gives a response deadline, and says the issuing bank's decision is final. A lost dispute leaves the processing fee unreimbursed. [Square, managing disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/3882-payment-disputes-walkthrough); [Square Payment Terms](https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/payment).
- Square requires evidence to be legible, verifiable, in English (or translated), and tied to the disputed transaction; its guidance identifies policy acceptance, receipt/description, communications, and proof of delivery/performance as relevant evidence. [Square, dispute-document guide](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7891-your-square-document-request-guide); [Square, unauthorized-charge disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8565-respond-to-disputes-for-unauthorized-charges).

**Reasonable risk-control recommendations:** preserve an immutable export/PDF of the event listing, checkout terms, buyer acceptance, payment/receipt, check-in or attendance evidence, cancellation/refund record, all buyer communications, dispute notices/evidence, and the settlement statement. The Pilot Event Agreement should make later chargebacks recoverable from the Settlement Recipient only to the extent counsel approves and the clause is clear, including a repayment due date and any future-settlement deduction method.

### 6. Federal reporting, withholding, worker status, and records

- Because LMNL is the Square participating payee, it may receive Form 1099-K for gross card payments. Payment-card reporting is not subject to the third-party-network dollar/count threshold, and the form's gross amount is reported without reducing for fees, refunds, discounts, or other adjustments. Receiving a 1099-K does not itself decide LMNL's income, deductions, or the Recipient Share's tax treatment. [IRS, Understanding Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k); [IRS Form 1099-K instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099k).
- If LMNL receives payments from a payment settlement entity on behalf of multiple participating payees and distributes them, federal law can treat LMNL as the payment settlement entity for downstream reporting. That result is another reason not to treat the pilot's manual Recipient Settlement as a casual onward payout. [26 U.S.C. §6050W](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A26+section%3A6050W+edition%3Aprelim%29); [IRS Form 1099-K instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099k).
- If the Recipient Settlement is payment for services performed for LMNL in its trade or business and meets Form 1099-NEC reporting rules, LMNL may need to obtain a Form W-9, issue Form 1099-NEC, and apply backup withholding when required. For tax years beginning after 2025, the current Form 1099-NEC threshold for qualifying nonemployee services is $2,000; reportable payments subject to backup withholding can require filing regardless of amount. [IRS Form 1099-NEC instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); [IRS, backup withholding](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/backup-withholding).
- A person is an employee or independent contractor based on the actual right of control and the whole relationship, not the title “Settlement Recipient” or “LMNL Admin.” If the payment is wages, payroll withholding and Form W-2 rules may apply instead of a 1099-NEC approach. [IRS, employee common-law rules](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employee-common-law-employee); [IRS, independent contractor defined](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-defined).
- Washington taxpayers must preserve suitable tax records, including returns, books, records, and invoices, for five years. Federal business records must support reported income, deductions, and credits and be kept for the applicable limitations period. [RCW 82.32.070](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=82.32.070); [IRS Publication 583](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583).

**CPA confirmation required:** whether the Recipient Share is compensation for services, a payment under a different income/right-to-proceeds arrangement, or wages; which information return (if any) is correct for the recipient's entity and tax status; whether backup withholding applies; and how LMNL's tax return reconciles Square's gross Form 1099-K amount to ticket taxes, refunds, fees, chargebacks, and Recipient Settlement.

## Pilot launch checklist

1. **No sales until** a Washington attorney confirms that LMNL is genuinely authorized to make the ticket sale in its own right and that the contemplated Recipient Settlement does not breach Square's third-party-funds prohibition; otherwise use a processor/merchant arrangement designed for the actual organizer-of-record model.
2. Have a Washington CPA classify the actual event and sales flow for B&O, sales tax, tax location, marketplace-facilitator status, and gross-receipts/Recipient-Share treatment; configure Square only after that answer.
3. Have a Washington attorney review the buyer terms and Pilot Event Agreement, especially the seller/principal wording, cancellation/refund promise, post-settlement chargeback recovery, money-transmitter/custody analysis, and federal payment-reporting role.
4. Ensure LMNL's business license/UBI, DOR registration, and required local endorsements are current; separately check the actual venue city's business-license, event, occupancy, liquor/food, and noise rules.
5. Make every advertised ticket price comply with 16 CFR Part 464; add tax/remittances explicitly ahead of the Net Ticket Revenue formula; do not distribute them as Recipient Share or LMNL Fee.
6. Obtain the Settlement Recipient's legal name, payment instructions, tax form (normally W-9 where appropriate), and signed Pilot Event Agreement before sales open.
7. Use a single event settlement packet: Square transaction export, fee/refund/dispute export, tax calculation/return support, settlement worksheet, approver, payment proof, and retained buyer/dispute evidence.
